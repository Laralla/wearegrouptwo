Spotlight: ASEAN

Southeast Asia is among the regions most exposed to climate change, and a living example of interdependence. Pick a member state.

🌫️ Haze Agreement

The ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution (2002) exists because smoke from fires in one country drifts into its neighbors.

⚡ ASEAN Power Grid

A long-term plan to connect national electricity grids so renewable power can be shared across borders.

🤝 Shared rivers & seas

The Mekong, monsoon rains and rising seas cross national lines, so no member can adapt alone.

🔗 Actors influence each other

Public pressure pushes governments to regulate. Regulation pushes companies toward clean tech. Cheaper clean tech makes stronger rules easier. When one moves, it pushes the next.

🚧 Why action stalls

Economic interests still favor fossil fuels. Free riders benefit when others cut emissions. Unequal responsibility: rich nations emitted most, yet poorer nations face the worst impacts. Short election cycles clash with decades-long plans.

Ways Forward

Tick the actions you'd support to see the collective impact.

Collective impact

Idea: polluters pay into an international fund, such as UNEP, based on the pollution they produce. That fund helps build green energy in developing countries that lack the money to cut emissions on their own.

🌱 Closing thought

In a world where countries are interconnected, global problems require us to look beyond individual interests and consider our shared responsibilities.
